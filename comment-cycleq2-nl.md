Setting, as in Chapter 4, §4.6: over the base $(c_1,c_2)$ the fibre is the $(3,3)$ surface $S$, equivalently the triple cover $t=\tau^3$ of the Weierstrass family $\eta^2=\xi^3+a(t)\xi+b(t)$ of `hodgepodgeconjecture`'s cycle-three model. $V_\omega$ is the nontrivial deck sector of $R^2$ containing the target, with Hodge numbers $(1,10,1)$.

**1. The sector on the fourfold.** With paired parity and numerator $h$-weight $5$ (the weight of $A$), the Fermat-ring count gives Hodge numbers $(18,78,18)$ for the cycle-q2 sector of $H^4$. It therefore contains $(3,1)$ classes.

**2. $V_\omega$ has no generic algebraic classes (modular, $p=1000003$).** As in the previous note, the $c$-derivatives of the holomorphic form $s$ span $V_\omega$: dimensions $1,3,6,10,12,12$ at orders $\le0,\dots,5$, at three base points. So $NS_\omega=0$ at the generic point.

**3. Noether–Lefschetz curves.**
- *Ordinary ones exist (exact, on the line $c_2=3$).* $\Delta=4a^3+27b^2$ is irreducible of degree 12 in $t$. Its $t$-discriminant has degree 444 in $c_1$ and factors as $c_1^{150}\cdot R_{38}^3\cdot N_{54}^2\cdot N_{72}$, where $R_{38}=\mathrm{Res}_t(a,b)$ is the cusp locus. Along $N_{54}$ and $N_{72}$ two $I_1$ fibres merge with $a,b\ne0$, so the Weierstrass surface gets an $A_1$ point. On the triple cover this happens at the three points $\tau^3=t_0$, permuted by the deck group, and each nontrivial sector gains one algebraic class. Their Gysin images on the fourfold are of type $(2,2)$.
- *No curve with a flat holomorphic form (modular).* A vertical divisor over a base curve $C$ contributes to $H^2(B,V_\omega)$ through flat sections of $V_\omega|_C$. A flat section of type $(2,0)$ forces Kodaira–Spencer to kill $s$ along the tangent of $C$, so $s,\nabla_1s,\nabla_2s$ must be dependent at every point of $C$. I computed that rank at every $(c_1,c_2)\in(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2$ for $p=101,211,307$. It equals $3$ at all $9900$, $43884$ and $93339$ points where the surface is quasi-smooth. The remaining $100$, $216$ and $297$ points lie on the discriminant, which the Griffiths-reduction engine cannot test. So there is no such curve in the quasi-smooth locus.

**4. Tentative consequence, with its gap.** Suppose a class $\alpha$ in this sector is supported on a divisor $D$. Over a small enough open $B^\circ$ of the base, only the horizontal components of $D$ remain: smooth families of curves $c:D^\circ\to B^\circ$ in the fibres. On Leray graded pieces their Gysin maps land in $NS_\omega\otimes H^*(B^\circ)=0$, since $R^1c_*\to R^3\pi_*=0$. If Gysin respected a Leray splitting, the image of the sector in $H^2(B^\circ,V_\omega)$ would vanish. By item 3, its $(3,1)$ part would then have to be supported over the discriminant curve or the coordinate boundary alone, which looks too small for an 18-dimensional $H^{3,1}$.

**The gap:** Gysin need not respect a splitting. Its off-diagonal component is a class in $H^1(B^\circ,\mathcal{Hom}(R^1c_*,V_\omega))$ and weights do not force it to vanish, so I do not claim the conclusion. If that component can be controlled, the paired-ratio fibration cannot carry this sector. A correction would then have to come from a different fibration, for instance threefold fibres over a curve with fibrewise coniveau one, as in Chapter 3B. If it cannot be controlled, that off-diagonal class is exactly where a correction must live: a family of fibre curves whose classes cancel in $NS$ but whose $H^1$ maps nontrivially.
