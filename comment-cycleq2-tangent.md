A solver and a low-degree exclusion for the canonical tangent equation of **cycle-q2** (the `CanonicalTangentSolution` interface of `benediamond`'s and `fqlx`'s draft solve), prepared with Claude Code assistance on behalf of `DiamondJM`. **No certificate, Lean proof, or solve PR is claimed.** The results are modular ($p=1000003$): evidence, not proof.

**1. The equation, as solved.** With $y=Y_2$, $g=F_y$, $T_i=g\partial_i-F_i\partial_y$ for $i<5$, $a=A/y$ and $e=X_0X_2^3Y_0+X_1^3X_2Y_1$, writing $t_i=P_i/H$ and clearing denominators in the rational form of the solution note gives
$$4g^2\sum_{i<5}\bigl(H\,T_i(P_i)-P_i\,T_i(H)\bigr)-H^2\,a\,(48y^5-e)\equiv0\pmod{F^2},\qquad\deg P_i=\deg H+2 .$$
I derived this directly from $v=\tfrac{A}{2g}\partial_y+\sum_it_iT_i$, for which $2v(F)=A$ and the condition is $\operatorname{div}v\equiv0\pmod{F^2}$. It is `CanonicalTangentClosureGoal` up to the normalization of the constants. A solution $(H,P)$ should therefore map to a `CanonicalTangentSolution`, with the quotient recovered by division; I have not run that through Lean.

**2. Three reductions that make it small.**
- *No quotient.* $F^2$ is monic of degree 12 in $y$. Reducing every term modulo $F^2$ in $y$ removes $S$, and the $P_i$ may be taken of $y$-degree below 12.
- *Characters.* The diagonal group fixing $F$ has order 432 including scalars (72 modulo scalars). For semi-invariant $H$ the unknown monomials of each $P_i$ lie in a single character class. A degree-6 test then has about 90 unknowns and takes 0.1 s. The unsplit six-variable system at $q=2$ exceeded 15 GB.
- *Shape of $H$.* Replacing $H$ by its norm in $y$ and then by the product of its group translates shows that $H$ may be taken free of $Y_2$ and semi-invariant, at the cost of multiplying its degree by up to $6\cdot72$.

**3. Exhaustive exclusion at low degree.** The $Y_2$-free monomials of degree $d$ fall into at most 72 character classes, of size at most 36 for $d\le12$. Write $H=\sum_jh_jm_j$ over one class. The equation is linear in the products $h_j\cdot(\text{coefficients of }P)$ and in the distinct products $h_jh_l$. If that linear relaxation has no solution with a nonzero product $h_jh_l$, no $H$ in the class solves the equation.

Result: **for every degree $d\le\,$9, every class is excluded.** No $Y_2$-free semi-invariant denominator of degree at most 9 solves the canonical tangent equation modulo $p$. Two controls: a source manufactured from random $P$ is reported solvable, and the real source at the same $H$ is not. The $s$-unknowns must be indexed by distinct monomial products: indexing by pairs leaves a spurious kernel vector at degree 6, from $X_0^6\cdot X_1^2Y_0^2Y_1^2=(X_0^3X_1Y_0Y_1)^2$.

Because of the degree factor in item 2, this excludes the natural low-degree supports in this presentation, not all supports of degree at most 9.

**4. Use.** `ct.solve(H)` tests any $Y_2$-free semi-invariant $H$ directly, so a candidate denominator from the saved current or the contact families can be checked in seconds to minutes; by my size estimate (not tested) dense linear algebra should reach degree about 18 on a 16 GB machine.
