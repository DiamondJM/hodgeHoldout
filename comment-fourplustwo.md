Computational notes for **four-plus-two-residual**: the four-variable core equation, denominator tests, and the size of the norm-contact family. Prepared with Claude Code assistance on behalf of `DiamondJM`. The protected $(F,q=1,A=X_0^3Y_0^3)$ is unchanged. **No certificate, Lean proof, or solve PR is claimed.** Existence for this target is the two-reflection theorem of Chapter 3B; these notes concern only the search for an explicit primitive. Items are labelled exact, modular or numerical.

**1. The core equation (exact; it is the suspension theorem of Chapter 2, §2.8 in homogeneous form).** Write $F=H(X_0,X_1,Y_0,Y_1)+B(X_2,Y_2)$. A rational vector field $p$ on $\mathbb C^4$, homogeneous of degree one, with
$$p(H)-\tfrac32\,H\,\operatorname{div}p=A\qquad(\ast)$$
lifts to
$$V=p+\frac{A-p(H)}{6B}\,(X_2\partial_{X_2}+Y_2\partial_{Y_2}),\qquad V(F)=A,\qquad A-V(F)+F\operatorname{div}V=-\frac{2}{3}\,\frac{(A-p(H))\,F^2}{HB}.$$
So $(\ast)$ gives a certificate with denominator $D_p\,H\,B$, where $D_p$ is the denominator of $p$. It comes from the weighted threefold $T:\ H+w^3=0$ by $w^3\mapsto B$ and $w\partial_w\mapsto\tfrac12(X_2\partial_{X_2}+Y_2\partial_{Y_2})$, which preserve both the action on the equation and the divergence. Conversely, expanding a $\mu_3$-equivariant certificate on $T$ to first order in $H+w^3$ gives $(\ast)$. Nothing depends on $B$ beyond its degree. I derived this independently before finding §2.8, and it agrees with the $5/3$ affine form there. The transfer was also checked at random rational points.

**2. Fixed-denominator tests of $(\ast)$ (modular, $p=268435399$).** For fixed $D$, $(\ast)$ is linear in the numerators, in four variables. Controls on the split core $h_X+h_Y$: $D=h_X$ solves the target $X_0^3X_1Y_0^2$ of $X$-degree four (the join field) and does not solve $X_0^3Y_0^3$. On the actual core, none of the following is solvable:
- 112 denominators of degree 1 to 12: all monomials of degree at most 4 with exponents at most 2, sums of sixth powers, $h_X$, $h_Y$, the coupling, $h_Xh_Y$, the partials of $H$, and binomials $Z_i^k\pm Z_j^k$ for $k=2,3,6$;
- 22 denominators $H^kD'$ with $k=1,2$ and $D'$ a small factor, of degree at most 18.

With the $Y$-parity split, degree about 35 is within reach of dense linear algebra on a small machine. The solver is `core4.py`.

**3. Norm contacts exist on this core, but the family is very large (numerical).** Eliminating $u=q(x)$ turns a norm-contact presentation of §3B.2 into
$$H+q^3=h\,\sigma(h)+Q\,M,$$
with $q,Q$ $\sigma$-even quadrics, $h$ a cubic and $M$ a $\sigma$-even quartic. After fixing the gauge ($Q[a^2]=1$, $q[a^2]=0$, no monomial of $h$ divisible by $a^2$, and $q[ab]=t_0$ as family parameter) this is 44 equations in 44 unknowns. Complex Newton iteration from random starts converges in about 80% of trials, to solutions with residual $10^{-14}$, nonsingular Jacobian and $Q$ of rank four. So the genus-four curves of Chapter 3B are present on this exact threefold.

At one value $t_0=0.37+0.21i$, however, 3065 converged starts gave 3065 pairwise distinct solutions. Under uniform sampling that puts the number of solutions above about $1.5\times10^6$ at the 95% level; non-uniform Newton basins only strengthen the bound. The parameter curve therefore has at least that many sheets over this gauge coordinate, and the surface swept by the full family has correspondingly large degree. Eliminating that surface and running the linear test of item 2 on it is not feasible. An explicit primitive along these lines would need a low-degree component of the parameter curve, or a different and smaller family of curves on $T$.
